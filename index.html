Geographic Context

The Iñupiat are the Inuit-Eskimo-speaking inhabitants of northern and northwestern Alaska, residing across roughly three dozen villages and towns from the Bering Strait to the Mackenzie Delta region (Burch, 1998). The stylized map below highlights the traditional Northwest Alaska homeland and several places referenced throughout this guide.

Cape Thompson & Cape Espenberg
Northern and southern coastal boundaries of the traditional Northwest Alaska Iñupiaq homeland along the Chukchi Sea (Burch, 1998).
Kotzebue Sound & Hotham Inlet
Central water bodies of the homeland into which the Noatak, Kobuk, and Selawik rivers drain (Burch, 1998).
Noatak & Kobuk Rivers
Major river systems forming the arterial routes of the Iñupiaq interior; documented in Burch (1998) as the primary highways of trade and seasonal movement.
Selawik
Site of the 2001 survival story: two students lost for seven days in a winter storm, surviving on skills taught by community elders (Dayo, 2001).
Arctic Coastal Plain & Travel Routes
The Colville, Meade, Utukok, and Kokolik rivers shown follow the major travel routes across the northern foothills of the Brooks Range documented by Burch (2005).
