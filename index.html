Geographic Context

The Iñupiat are the Inuit-Eskimo-speaking inhabitants of northern and northwestern Alaska, residing across roughly three dozen villages and towns from the Bering Strait to the Mackenzie Delta region (Burch, 1998). The stylized map below highlights the traditional Northwest Alaska homeland and several places referenced throughout this guide.

Stylized reference map, not drawn to scale. Shaded region approximates the traditional Northwest Alaska homeland described by Burch (1998), bounded by the Chukchi Sea coastline between Cape Thompson and Cape Espenberg.

Cape Thompson & Cape Espenberg
Mark the historic north and south boundaries of the Iñupiaq homeland along the Chukchi Sea coast (Burch, 1998).
Kotzebue Sound
Traditional Iñupiaq waters at the heart of the Northwest Alaska region (Burch, 1998).
Selawik
Site of the 2001 survival story that inspired that year's Iñupiaq Days theme, "Arctic Survival" (Dayo, 2001).
Fairbanks
Home of the University of Alaska Press, publisher of Burch's (1998) ethnography of the Iñupiaq nations.
Anchorage
Headquarters of the Cook Inlet Tribal Council, founding partner behind Never Alone ("The Making of Never Alone," 2019).
